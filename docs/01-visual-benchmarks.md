# Visual benchmarks: what the best explainers do, and what we adopt

The brief was to look at how asset managers and others explain products visually, keep the best of it, and adapt it for a Ram Investments diary. This note lists what we looked at, what each does well, and where that idea shows up in the diary pages.

## The short version

The strongest product explainers share four habits:

1. **One fixed template, repeated.** Readers learn the layout once, then compare products by looking at the same spot on each page. Regulators impose this on purpose (EU key information documents, SEBI factsheets, IRDAI's customer information sheet).
2. **The regulator's own risk scale, not a home-made score.** Investors already see the SEBI riskometer and credit ratings. Reusing them is clearer and safer than a new "risk out of 5".
3. **One picture of the mechanism per product,** usually with a named person and real numbers, instead of decorative imagery.
4. **Saying who a product is not for.** Disclosure regimes in the EU and Australia require it, and it builds trust.

We combined these into a **field guide**: each product is an "entry" with the same two pages. The left page helps you recognise it; the right page helps you decide whether it fits. The diary format adds one thing brochures cannot: space to write your own numbers next to the explanation.

## What we chose not to do

- **"Return potential" stars or scores.** They imply returns, invite compliance problems and are subjective. Risk is shown only on scales the regulators define.
- **Radar or spider charts.** Hard to read at A5 and easy to misread.
- **Returns of any kind, past performance, projections and premium quotes.** We cannot give returns or say what an investment will grow to, so no page does (house rule; see [02-framework.md](02-framework.md), section 5). Industry size appears only on the two in-depth spreads, dated and sourced.
- **Stock photography.** It adds no information. Every graphic explains a mechanism.
- **Scheme, fund or insurer names.** The pages explain categories, which keeps them in investor-education territory and stops them from going stale.

## How Indian insurers talk about protection without talking about death

Researched for the term and health pages. Indian buyers avoid discussing death, and the most successful insurers have learned to sell protection around it.

| Who | What they do | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|
| LIC, "Zindagi ke saath bhi, zindagi ke baad bhi" (Mudra, 1999) | Written because LIC could neither sell a savings product nor "focus on death, that would be too harsh" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/40-years-ago-and-now-advertising-helped-insure-lic-against-competition-114121700166_1.html)) | Talk about life and the family, before and after |
| LIC's later campaign (JWT) | Dropped the widow, "Mrs Sharma", and moved to the policyholder's responsibility to the family ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/28352_lic-shifting-focus-from-death-to-life); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/lic-buries-death-a-bit-too-late/9455.html)) | The reader is the hero who looks after the family, not the person who is lost |
| HDFC Life, "First Call" (2025) and its 2026 preparedness campaign | Protection shown as the same care as the first call to family in a vulnerable moment; "family security even in our absence" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/hdfc-life-launches-first-call-campaign-on-family-protection-10513562); [Impact](https://www.impactonnet.com/more-from-impact/hdfc-life-spotlights-financial-preparedness-in-latest-campaign-16246.html)) | "In your absence", "if something happens to you" |
| Policybazaar with Pankaj Tripathi | Humour: putting off term cover is a "ghor paap" against the family ([Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/policybazaar-unveils-new-term-life-ad-campaign-featuring-pankaj-tripathi-220704/)) | Gentle nudge against delay, never fear |
| Aegon Religare, #NothingWillHappen (2015) | Satirised reliance on "buri nazar" and good karma instead of cover ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/digital/43666_aegon-religare-better-safe-than-sorry); [Campaign India](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/aegon-takes-humourous-route-to-blast-nothingwillhappen-myth/4gd4n3cv5ndb1114wxjh5k9sd9)) | Optimism is normal; answer it with questions, not warnings |
| Marketing commentary | The most effective campaigns "focus on love, not loss, and empowerment more than fear" ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/guest-post/marketing-mortality-walking-line-between-emotion-ethics-9623543)) | The tone rule for every Protect page |
