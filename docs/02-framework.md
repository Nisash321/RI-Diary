# The framework: "Know Your Products"

## In one paragraph

The product section is a field guide. Eight products sit under four jobs a client's money can do: **Protect, Build, Specialise, Globalise.** Every product gets the same two-page entry: the left page helps the reader recognise it, the right page helps them decide whether it fits. Two products the firm wants clients to understand in depth, SIF and AIF, get a second spread straight after their entry. Risk is shown only on scales the regulators already use, and no page shows or projects returns. Each entry ends with space for the client's own numbers, which turns a brochure into a page they keep using all year.

28 pages make one 16-page and one 12-page signature, or a single insert on heavier paper. Page numbers are worked out by the build, so adding or removing a spread never leaves a stale cross-reference.

## 2. The four jobs

| Job | Products | The client's question | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protect | Term insurance, health insurance | "If something happens to me, is my family covered?" | Neem green |
| Build | Mutual funds, SIF, NCDs | "How do I grow wealth and earn an income?" | Neel indigo |
| Specialise | PMS, AIF | "My portfolio is large. What more can I do?" | Jamun |
| Globalise | GIFT City | "How do I invest beyond India?" | Haldi |

**Why this order.** Protection comes before investing, which is standard financial-planning practice and the order a regulator would expect a distributor to follow.

**Why SIF sits in Build, next to mutual funds.** A SIF is run by a mutual fund house under the mutual fund regulations and is taxed like a mutual fund. It can be a whole portfolio for someone with no mutual funds (an asset allocator strategy), the conservative part of a portfolio (hybrid and debt strategies), or the equity part (equity long-short). Placing it straight after mutual funds means a mutual fund investor reads it next and sees it as part of the same portfolio, not as a step "on top of" their funds. The entry never describes a SIF as an add-on or as riskier by nature: the Risk Band, not the wrapper, says how adventurous a strategy is.

Within Specialise, products climb by minimum ticket: PMS (₹50 lakh, or ₹25 lakh on the PRIM route from April 2027), then AIF (₹1 crore). Globalise comes last because it is a route that can hold many kinds of assets, not an asset class of its own.

The job, not the product, sets the colour, so a reader can tell at a glance which part of their plan a page belongs to.

## 3. Anatomy of an entry

### Left page: recognise it

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Entry number and job chip | Number in the job colour; job named in the running head |
| Title and formal name | The name clients use, then the formal name and regulator |
| In one line | Two or three sentences. No jargon that is not explained in the same sentence |
| How it works (the plate) | One diagram of the mechanism, ideally with a worked example. Drawn to scale or marked "not to scale" or "illustration" |
| How to recognise it | Three features that set the product apart. No numbers that repeat "At a glance" |
| Often confused with | The look-alike and the one-line difference |

### Right page: decide if it fits

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| At a glance | The same six rows for every product: Regulator, Minimum (or What you pay), Time commitment, Money out (or When it pays), Costs, Tax in brief |
| Ticket ruler | For investments, the minimum on one log scale from ₹100 to ₹1 crore, identical on every page |
| The check that matters most | The regulator's own scale where one exists (riskometer, credit rating). Otherwise the single most useful check: cover worksheet, fine-print table, tax wrapper, concentration, profit waterfall, currency effect |
| Suits you if / Think twice if | Three each. "Think twice" names the people the product does not suit |
| Ask before you sign | Four questions with tick boxes |
| My notes | Four write-in fields specific to the product |
| Legal line | The exact standard warning, plus the registration line for that product |

### In-depth spreads

An in-depth spread follows its entry, keeps the entry's job colour and number ("04 in depth"), and has no write-in fields. It is for ideas a client needs to understand completely, not for more facts. Each one ends with its own legal line.

| Spread | What it must get across | How |
|---|---|---|
| SIF in depth (pp.12–13) | A SIF borrows abilities from PMS and Category III AIFs while keeping the mutual fund structure and tax treatment; it covers conservative to adventurous strategies; it works in rising, falling and sideways markets | Ability matrix (MF, SIF, PMS, Cat III AIF); the seven SEBI strategies on one conservative-to-adventurous line; three ways to use a SIF, including as a whole portfolio; a timeline of when tax is paid in each wrapper; industry growth |
| AIF in depth (pp.20–21) | Companies now stay private for a decade or more, so much of their early growth happens before an IPO; AIFs are how eligible investors take part; results vary widely, so the manager matters | A company's life before and after listing; three dated facts (age at listing, private funding, share of large companies that are private); which AIF categories invest at each stage; a plain-words caution; an "Is it for you?" checklist |

## 4. Visual system

- **Page:** A5, 148 × 210 mm. Margins 11 mm top and outer, 12 mm bottom, 15 mm inside for the binding. A5 and B5 share the same proportions, so the whole section scales to B5 without re-layout; B5 gives older readers 19% larger type.
- **Type:** Anek Latin for headings and labels, Mukta for reading. Both are from Ek Type, an Indian foundry, carry the ₹ sign, and have Devanagari and Tamil companions for a regional-language edition. Both are free to print under the SIL Open Font License.
- **Sizes:** body 8.6 pt, lead sentences 10.2 pt, smallest legal text 6.3 pt. On B5 these become about 10.2, 12.1 and 7.5 pt.
- **Colour:** four job colours plus their tints, a warm near-black, and a separate SEBI-style green-to-red ramp used only for risk scales. Job colours never carry risk meaning.
- **Charts:** marks drawn to scale (the exposure bars, the ticket ladder on a ten-times scale, the tax bars). Anything schematic says "not to scale" or "illustration".
- **No photographs, emoji or decorative icons.** Every graphic explains something.

## 5. Writing rules

- Plain words, short sentences, active voice. Explain a term the first time it appears, then use it consistently.
- Indian number formats: ₹10 lakh, ₹1 crore, USD 2,50,000 (RBI style).
- Typical values say "often" or "usually". Rules and limits are stated flatly and dated.
- Every page carries "Facts as of October 2026".

### House rule: no returns

We do not give returns or say what an investment will grow to. That rules out, on every page:

- return figures of any kind: past, typical, "illustrative" or assumed (no "at 10% a year", no "8% in US dollars");
- projections or growth illustrations ("₹10 lakh becomes ₹16 lakh", a savings curve rising over time, maturity values worked out from a rate);
- comparisons of how one product or category has performed against another or against an index, including dispersion figures quoted in return points;
- multiples that read like results ("3.6×"), even when they describe something else.

What we can show: rules and limits, minimums, costs, tax rates and when tax is paid, ages and timelines, how money moves in and out, and dated industry facts (assets, commitments, funding), each with its source and clearly labelled as industry size. Where a mechanism needs a picture of money coming back, show both outcomes (the AIF cash curve has a dashed "less than you put in" path) and no amounts.

Clients still ask about returns. The pages give them the questions to ask instead ("How has the strategy done against its benchmark over a full market cycle?"), and the answer comes from the provider's own documents.

### Insurance tone: love, not loss

Indian buyers avoid talking about death, and insurers have learned to sell protection without dwelling on it (see [01-visual-benchmarks.md](01-visual-benchmarks.md)). The Protect pages follow the same rules:

- Write about the family's plans, not the death: "keeps your family's plans on track if you are not there to provide for them", "if something happens to you", "in your absence". The words "die" and "death" do not appear on the pages.
- Show what the cover protects (the home loan, the children's education, the household's monthly income), not what is lost.
- End the story well. The term plate closes on "Job done" at 60: the loan is repaid, the children are independent, and paying only for protection kept the premium low.
- Treat optimism ("nothing will happen to me") as normal, not as a mistake to correct. Ask questions instead of warning.
- Frame disclosure as help ("Honest disclosure keeps claims smooth"), not as a threat.
- Health insurance is about recovery: the insurer pays "so the focus stays on getting better".

## 6. Compliance built into the design

- The section explains **categories**, never named schemes, funds, insurers or issuers, so it reads as investor education.
- Standard warnings are printed **verbatim** in bold where a regulator prescribes them.
- Each entry carries the registration line for that product (ARN, SIFD, APRN, IRDAI registration and so on).
- The AIF entry and the AIF in-depth spread state that AIFs are offered only by private placement to eligible investors, and that the page is not an offer. The private-markets case is made with structural facts (age at listing, private funding, the share of companies that are private), never with returns, and is balanced on the same spread by "results vary widely", "Is it for you?" and the risk that companies never list.
- SIF tax statements (highest-bracket rates, when tax is paid) need sign-off from a tax adviser before print.
- Insurance pages need written approval from each insurer whose products are distributed (IRDAI advertising rules).
- See [03-fact-sheet.md](03-fact-sheet.md) for every claim and its source.

## 7. Changing or adding a product

All entry copy lives in `product-section/src/content/products.mjs`. Each product's diagram is in `src/plates/<id>.svg` and its gauge in `src/gauges/<id>.html`. An in-depth spread is an HTML file in `src/pages/` named in the product's `extra` field. Front and back matter refer to pages with `{{pg:<section id>}}`, which the build replaces with the right page number.

Run `node product-section/build.mjs --render` to rebuild the HTML, the proof PDF and the spread previews. The build warns if any text runs off a page, past the side margins or into the folio, or if the side-by-side rows stop lining up.

To add a ninth product (for example NPS or bonds), add an object to `products`, a plate and a gauge, and extend the contents, the four-jobs page and the side-by-side spread in `src/pages/`.
