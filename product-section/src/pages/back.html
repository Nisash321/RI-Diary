Words to know

Terms used in this guide, in plain words.

Accredited investor. An investor certified as meeting set income or wealth tests. Some products then allow lower minimums.

AMC. Asset management company: the fund house that runs mutual funds and SIFs.

Arbitrage. Buying a share and selling its futures contract at the same time, to earn the small price gap.

Asset cover. How much the assets backing a secured NCD are worth, compared with what is owed.

Benchmark. A market index used to judge how well a manager has done.

Capital call. A request for part of the money you committed to an AIF. Also called a drawdown.

Carried interest. The AIF manager’s share of profits above the hurdle.

Co-payment. The share of each health claim you pay yourself.

Credit rating. An agency’s opinion of how likely a borrower is to pay on time, from AAA to D.

Deductible. The part of a year’s bills that a top-up policy does not pay.

Demat account. An account that holds your shares, bonds and NCDs in electronic form.

Derivatives. Futures and options: contracts whose value moves with a share or an index.

Equity-oriented. A fund or strategy with 65% or more in Indian shares, taxed like an equity fund.

Exit load. A charge for selling mutual fund units within a set period.

Exposure. How much of a fund moves with the market. A SIF can take it below zero.

Hedge. A position that rises when your holdings fall, to cushion the loss.

High-water mark. A rule that a performance fee is paid only on gains above the portfolio’s previous highest value.

Hurdle. The minimum profit investors must receive before the manager shares in profits.

IPO. Initial public offer: when a company first sells shares to the public and lists on a stock exchange.

KYC. Know your customer: the identity and address check every investor completes.

Long-short. Owning shares expected to rise, and betting against those expected to fall.

LRS. Liberalised Remittance Scheme: up to USD 2,50,000 abroad per person, per financial year.

LTCG and STCG. Long- and short-term capital gains tax on profit when you sell, redeem or switch. For equity, within 12 months is short-term.

Moratorium. After 5 years of continuous health cover, a claim cannot be refused for a health detail left out, except in fraud.

NAV. Net asset value: the price of one mutual fund unit, worked out every business day.

NBFC. Non-banking financial company: a finance company that is not a bank.

Nominee. The person you name to receive the money from a policy or investment in your absence.

Old tax regime. The older way of filing tax, with deductions such as for premiums. The new regime applies unless you choose the old one.

Pass-through. Income mostly taxed in your hands, not in the fund, as for AIF Category I and II.

Portfolio. All your investments together. Your core portfolio is the main, steady part your goals rely on.

PRIM. A new kind of PMS, from 1 April 2027, that invests only in mutual funds, from ₹25 lakh.

Private equity. Funds that buy stakes in growing private companies.

Private markets. Companies and assets not listed on a stock exchange.

Redeem, switch. Redeem: take your money out of a fund. Switch: move it to another fund or strategy.

REIT, InvIT. Listed trusts that own rental property (REIT) or roads, power lines and similar assets (InvIT).

Risk Band. The risk label for every SIF strategy, from Level 1 (lowest) to Level 5 (highest).

Riskometer. SEBI’s six-level risk label shown on every mutual fund scheme.

Short position. A holding that gains when a share or index falls.

SIP. Systematic investment plan: a fixed amount invested at regular intervals.

Slab rate. Your income-tax rate, the same rate you pay on income from salary or business.

Sum assured, sum insured. What a life policy pays on a claim; the most a health policy pays in a year.

TCS. Tax collected at source: tax your bank collects when you send large amounts abroad. It is adjusted against your tax.

TER. Total expense ratio: a fund’s yearly running costs, taken from the NAV. SEBI caps its base part.

Venture capital. Money for young, fast-growing private companies, years before they list.

Waiting period. The time after you buy health cover before some illnesses are covered.
