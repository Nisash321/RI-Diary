Words to know

Terms used in this guide, in plain words.

Accredited investor. Certified as meeting income or wealth tests; some products then have lower minimums.

AMC. Asset management company: the firm that runs a mutual fund’s schemes.

Arbitrage. Buying a share and selling its future at the same time, to capture the small price gap.

Asset cover. The value of assets backing a secured NCD, compared with what is owed.

Capital call. A request for part of the money you committed to an AIF. Also called a drawdown.

Carried interest. The AIF manager’s share of profits above the hurdle.

Cashless claim. The insurer pays a network hospital directly, so you do not pay up front.

Co-payment. The share of each health claim you pay yourself.

Credit rating. An agency’s opinion of how likely a borrower is to pay on time, from AAA to D.

Deductible. The part of a year’s bills that a top-up policy does not pay.

Derivatives. Futures and options: contracts whose value moves with a share or an index.

Exit load. A charge for selling mutual fund units before a set period.

Exposure. How much of a fund moves with the market. A SIF can take it below zero.

Free look. 30 days to return a new insurance policy if it does not suit you.

Hedge. A position that rises when your holdings fall, to cushion the loss.

High-water mark. A performance fee is paid only on gains above the portfolio’s previous peak.

Hurdle rate. The return investors must receive before the manager shares in profits.

IFSCA. International Financial Services Centres Authority, the regulator in GIFT City.

Long-short. Owning shares expected to rise, and betting against those expected to fall.

LRS. Liberalised Remittance Scheme: up to USD 2,50,000 abroad per person, per financial year.

Moratorium. After 5 continuous years of health cover, a claim cannot be refused for non-disclosure, except fraud.

NAV. Net asset value: the price of one mutual fund unit, worked out every business day.

Nominee. The person you name to receive the money from a policy or investment in your absence.

Pass-through. Income taxed in your hands, not in the fund, as for AIF Category I and II.

Portability. Moving a health policy to another insurer while keeping waiting-period credit.

Private markets. Companies and assets not listed on a stock exchange.

Rider. An add-on to an insurance policy, such as cover for critical illness or accidents.

Risk band. The five-level risk label shown for every SIF strategy.

Riskometer. SEBI’s six-level risk label shown on every mutual fund scheme.

SIP. Systematic investment plan: a fixed amount invested at regular intervals.

Sum assured, sum insured. What a life policy pays on a claim; the most a health policy pays in a year.

TCS. Tax collected at source by your bank on large overseas remittances, adjusted against your tax.

TER. Total expense ratio: a fund’s yearly running costs, taken from the NAV.

Venture capital. Money for young, fast-growing private companies, years before they list.

Waiting period. Time after buying health cover before some conditions are covered.
