SIF in depth

What a SIF manager can do

Fund houses explain SIFs with simple examples like these. Each shows one tool a SIF can use that an equity mutual fund cannot, or can use only in part.

Markets fall

Protect the portfolio

  1. What happensA sudden shock threatens to pull the whole market down.
  2. What the manager doesSells index futures against part of the fund. This is a hedge.
  3. What can followIf the market falls, the hedge gains and offsets part of the loss. If it does not, the hedge costs a little.

One weak company

Gain from its fall

  1. What happensA company looks overpriced, and its business is weakening.
  2. What the manager doesTakes a short position in its futures, for a small part of the fund.
  3. What can followIf the price falls, the fund gains. If it rises, that position loses. An equity mutual fund cannot do this.

Markets go sideways

Earn a price gap

  1. What happensA share and its one-month future trade at slightly different prices.
  2. What the manager doesBuys the share and sells the future together. This is arbitrage.
  3. What can followAt expiry the two prices meet, so the gap is earned whether the share rises, stays or falls, less costs.

Hypothetical examples, to show how the tools work. Real strategies differ, and the manager’s calls can be wrong.

Using a SIF in your portfolio

Seven strategies, from cautious to adventurousWhere each one usually sits

The usual order only. What counts is each strategy’s Risk Band, from Level 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest).

Three ways to use a SIFMixes shown are examples

As your whole portfolio

No mutual funds yet, or want one simple fund? An asset allocator strategy moves money across shares, bonds, gold and REITs.

As the steady part

Hybrid strategies hold much of the money in arbitrage and bonds, with a smaller part in shares, aiming for a steadier ride.

As the equity part

Equity long-short strategies can sit beside your equity funds. They can hedge, or cut exposure, when markets fall.
